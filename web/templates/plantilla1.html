Problem Statement & Public Health

Relevance of the Need

Addressing the critical upper-body safety gap in daily urban navigation for individuals with severe visual impairments.

A Global Public Health Priority

According to the World Health Organization (WHO), at least 2.2 billion people worldwide experience near or distance vision impairment, with approximately 43 million individuals living with total blindness.

While the traditional white cane remains an indispensable tool for detecting ground-level hazards such as curbs, steps, and surface irregularities, its physical reach is restricted to floor level. Consequently, users remain exposed to severe upper-body and head collisions.

Common Unprotected Urban Hazards

  • Low-hanging tree branches encroaching on pedestrian sidewalks
  • Wall-mounted street signs, commercial awnings, and scaffolding
  • Open vehicle tailgates, truck side-mirrors, and ground-level open windows
